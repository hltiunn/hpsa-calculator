🏥 HPSA Designation Calculator

Explore how NP/PA FTE equivalency and panel size assumptions change Health Professional Shortage Area designations

📖 How to Use This Tool

  1. Start with a preset (green box below) or enter your own service area data on the left panel.
  2. Adjust the sliders to change assumptions — NP/PA FTE weight controls how much each NP or PA "counts" relative to a physician (0 = excluded like current law; 1.0 = fully equivalent).
  3. Read the results on the right — see side-by-side comparison of current law vs. your assumptions, including the population-to-provider ratio, HRSA severity score, and whether the area would be designated as an HPSA.
  4. Check the sensitivity tables at the bottom right — they show how designation status flips across a range of NP weights, so you can identify the "tipping point" where an area gains or loses HPSA status.

💡 Key question this tool answers: "If we counted NPs/PAs in the HPSA formula, how many shortage areas would remain?" Under current law (42 CFR Part 5), only physicians are counted. Wittkower et al. (2026) found that adding NPs eliminated ~80% of HPSA designations.

📚 Data Sources (Three-Tier Hierarchy)

Tier 1: Government / Regulatory (42 CFR Part 5)

  • 42 CFR Appendix A to Part 5 — Criteria for designation: pop/FTE physician ratio ≥3,500:1 (or ≥3,000:1 with high needs). Only MDs/DOs counted. NPs/PAs explicitly excluded.
  • HRSA Scoring — HPSA scores 0-25; used for NHSC placement priority.
  • HRSA Supply Modeling — 1 FTE = 40 hours/week clinical + non-clinical. Interns = 0.1 FTE; foreign grads w/o unrestricted license = 0.5 FTE.
  • 42 U.S.C. §254e — Mandates annual HPSA review.

Tier 2: Peer-Reviewed Literature

  • Wittkower, Bradley & Plemmons (MCRR, 2026) — "Including Nurse Practitioners in the Calculation of Primary Care HPSAs." DOI: 10.1177/10775587261442035. Key findings: 238,915 validated primary care NPs from NPPES+licensure matching (Oct 2025). At 65% FT (rest 0.5 FTE) and 80% own-panel, 80.2% of 970 HPSA counties no longer met threshold (3,000:1). At 100% additive: 85.2%. Uses HRSA severity scoring (0–5 double-weighted; max 25 total).
  • Morgan et al. (JAAPA, 2021) — NP+PA combined productivity ratio vs physicians: 0.85 (2016) → 0.88 (2019). PAs nearly equivalent to family physicians in visits/FTE.
  • Bae (JAANP, 2016) — NP average 80 patients/week; panel size avg 567 (vs physician ~2,000).
  • Mayo-Smith et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2022) — Systematic review: NP-to-physician panel size ratios range 0.5 to 1.0.
  • Martsolf et al. (JNP, 2022) — NP independent panel management determinants; lower proportion under graded independence measure.
  • Sun et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2024) — 5 distinct FTE staffing patterns in health centers; quality varies by APRN/PA proportion.
  • UW CHWS — PA overall physician FTE estimate: 0.83 (based on 61.4 vs 74.2 visits/week).

Tier 3: Research Institutes / Organizations

  • Paragon Health Institute (2024) — "Where Are Provider Shortages?" Argues HPSA methodology frozen in 1970s; ~600,000 NPs+PAs excluded. Tioga/Potter County example: adding 2 FTE NP/PA drops ratio from 4,723:1 → 2,361:1, removing HPSA status.
  • HRSA 2008 NPRM — If NPs/PAs included, only 38% of MUA/Ps would retain designation.
  • HRSA 2011 NRMC — Proposed including NPs/PAs; 12M people would lose HPSA coverage, 20.5M newly eligible (net +8.5M). Never adopted.
  • GAO (1995, 2006) — Criticized failure to update HPSA/MUA lists and methodology.
  • Graham Center (AAFP) — Maps using 1:3,500 physician-to-population ratio for HPSA visualization.
  • AANP Workforce Survey (2024) — 65% primary care NPs full-time; 88% have own panel.
  • HRSA NSSRN (2022 data, released 2024) — 80% of primary care NPs full-time; 80% have own panel. Part-time avg 21–23 hrs/wk.
  • NCCPA (2025) — 189,907 PAs; 22% (~41,780) in primary care.
